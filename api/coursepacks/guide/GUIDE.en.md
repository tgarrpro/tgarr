# Publishing a course in @tgarr0

This channel holds courses made by volunteers, in every language, for families who use tgarr - a free program that
turns a computer at home into a media center and learning library for children. A course posted here can be added
in tgarr with one click; the lessons are then fetched from this channel to the family's own computer.

Please read this page before you post.

## 1. What may be posted

**Only material you have the right to share freely:**

- what you made yourself - your own recordings, videos, texts, worksheets;
- what is in the public domain;
- what its maker published under a Creative Commons licence (say which, and where it comes from).

**Not** material somebody else holds the rights to: courses you bought, recordings of lessons from a paid platform,
scans of school books that are still in copyright, films and series.

Everything posted here is published under **CC BY-SA 4.0** unless your post says otherwise: anybody may use, copy
and adapt it, if they name you and share what they make of it in the same way. If you want another Creative Commons
licence for your course, say so in its first post.

Courses are for children. Nothing sexual, no cruelty for its own sake, no hate, no advertising, and no preaching
of a religion or a party.

## 2. How to post the lessons

- **One file in one post.** A video, a recording, a PDF, an e-book.
- **The name of the file is the name of the lesson.** Begin it with a number so that the order is plain:
  `01 The alphabet.mp4`, `02 Vowels.mp4`.
- **Put the course's tag in the caption of every post.** The tag begins with the language of the course, then a
  short name, then a number if there will be more parts: `#en_phonics1`, `#de_lesen1`, `#zh_pinyin1`,
  `#es_lectura2`. One tag is one course. Do not use somebody else's tag.
- **Post the lessons in the order they are to be taken.** tgarr shows them in the order they were posted.
- A file may be up to 2 GB. Prefer MP4 (H.264) for video, MP3 or M4A for sound, PDF or EPUB for text: every
  device can open those.
- No names, faces or voices of children who are not your own, and no addresses or phone numbers.

## 3. Announce the course

When the lessons are posted, post one more message - the **course card** - with the same tag and these lines:

```
#en_phonics1
Title: Phonics, part 1
About: Thirty short lessons on the sounds of English letters.
Language: en
Ages: 5-8            (5-8, 8-12, 12-16, 16+ or all)
Subject: reading     (reading, language, math, science, history, arts, music, computing, life, other)
Licence: CC BY-SA 4.0
By: your name, or the name you want to be credited with
```

`reading` is reading in one's own language; `language` is learning a foreign one.

## 4. What happens then

The course is read by the maintainers: that the licence is stated, and that nothing in it is unfit for a
children's library. Then it is listed in tgarr and families can add it. This is done by hand and can take some
days.

**Lessons you add later do not appear by themselves.** A course is listed up to the last post that was read. When
you have added lessons, post a short message with the tag saying so; they are read, and then they appear. This is
so that nothing can reach a child's screen that nobody has looked at.

A course that turns out not to be free to share, or not fit for children, is taken off the list.

## 5. If you know GitHub

You can write the list file yourself and send it in: <https://github.com/tgarrpro/tgarr>, folder
`api/coursepacks/` - the guide there says how.

Thank you for making something for other people's children.
